I am an AI/ML and robotics researcher with a focus on robot learning, human-robot interaction (HRI), reinforcement learning (RL), and human-centered AI. I earned my Ph.D. from Carnegie Mellon University, co-advised by Dr. Daniel Cardoso Llach (Computational Design) and Dr. Jean Oh (Robotics). My passion lies in creating robots that understand and consider human presence and intentions. A defining moment was a night at Mill-19 when my autonomous robot, Husky, subtly adjusted its path to avoid disturbing me while I was deep in thought—an unexpected display of sensitivity from a finely tuned RL model. This experience cemented my dedication to developing empathetic and human-centered robots.

Currently, I leverage my interdisciplinary expertise in machine learning, computational design, and robotics to create AI-driven robots that support people in labor-intensive tasks. A key project from my Ph.D. involved designing an RL-driven "work companion rover" to assist workers on construction sites with practical, context-aware support. My inspiration for developing empathetic companion robots is fueled by characters like TARS from "Interstellar", Benben from "The Wandering Earth II", and Baymax from "Big Hero 6".

Outside of academia, I enjoy photography, cooking, reading, drumming, and caring for my two cats, Sophie (Tangtang) and Huihui. A favorite book of mine is Herbert Simon's "The Sciences of the Artificial", which significantly influenced my decision to pursue a Ph.D. at Carnegie Mellon.
