ABOUT ME

“AI systems are artificial artifacts designed to exhibit intelligent behavior.” This insight from Herbert Simon captures the essence of my research. With both a researcher’s precision and a designer’s intuition, I’m committed to the art and science of creating intelligent agents, algorithms, and systems that understand, learn from, adapt to, and ultimately benefit humanity. My work spans AI/ML, computational design, and robotics, with a particular focus on reinforcement learning (RL), human-centered AI, and human-AI interaction. At its core, my research explores how collaborative systems can perceive, reason about, and respond to real-world human intentions and needs.

I am currently a Sr. Applied Scientist at Amazon, where I focus on user-centric optimization and intent alignment in large-scale AI/ML systems, leveraging state-of-the-art approaches in foundation models and adaptive learning. Previously, I completed my Ph.D. at Carnegie Mellon University, co-advised by Dr. Daniel Cardoso Llach (Computational Design) and Dr. Jean Oh (Robotics). My doctoral research centered on designing and training embodied agents that actively support and collaborate with humans in physically demanding work environments. A central project involved prototyping a reinforcement learning-driven “work companion rover”—an adaptive, socially aware robotic co-pilot that assisted human workers by transporting heavy tools and materials, and reducing physical strain in dynamic work settings. As a highlight, the robot navigated cluttered, high-traffic environments with social awareness, learning to recognize and adapt to workers’ unique behaviors through incremental alignment. The project was supported by the State of Pennsylvania, Autodesk, Google, and CMU MFI—and most meaningfully, by direct collaboration with frontline workers and field practitioners, reinforcing my belief in the real-world agency of human-AI collaborative systems. As a sci-fi enthusiast, my vision for such collaboration is also shaped by portrayals of empathetic machines like TARS in Interstellar, Baymax in Big Hero 6, and Benben in The Wandering Earth II, which imagine AI not just as tools, but as thoughtful partners in human-centered contexts.

In parallel, my research—situated at the intersection of AI/ML, computation, and design—also explores the development of AI agents and algorithms with spatial awareness, sense, and creativity. I am particularly interested in incentivizing these capabilities through mechanisms such as active learning and human-AI interaction.
