## 1. Setting

Let $\{E, (\mathbb{P}_{\theta})_{\theta\in\Theta}\}$ be a statistical model with a sample of $n$ i.i.d. r.v. $X_1, X_2, ..., X_n$. Assume that $\theta^*\in\Theta$ is the true parameter.

Our goal is to find an estimator $\hat\theta=\hat\theta(X_1, X_2, ..., X_n)$ s.t. $\mathbb{P}_{\theta}$ is close to $\mathbb{P}_{\theta^*}$ for the true parameter $\theta^*$

This also means, 
$$\forall A\subset E, |\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(A) - \mathbb{P}_{\theta^*}(A)|\text{ is small}$$


> The two curves are as close as possible.

## 2.Measures

### 2.1 Total Variace

$$TV(\mathbb{P}_{\theta}, \mathbb{P}_{\theta^*})=\max_{A\subset E}|\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(A) - \mathbb{P}_{\theta^*}(A)|$$

#### 2.1.1Discreet & continuous case
$$TV(\mathbb{P}_{\theta}, \mathbb{P}_{\theta^*})=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{x\in E}|p_{\theta}(x) - p_{\theta^*}(x)|$$
$$TV(\mathbb{P}_{\theta}, \mathbb{P}_{\theta^*})=\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{x\in E}|f_{\theta}(x) - f_{\theta^*}(x)|dx$$

#### 2.1.2 Properties
- Symmetry. 
  $$TV(\mathbb{P}_{\theta}, \mathbb{P}_{\theta^*})=TV(\mathbb{P}_{\theta^*}, \mathbb{P}_{\theta})$$
- Positive.
  $$0 \leq TV(\mathbb{P}_{\theta}, \mathbb{P}_{\theta^*})\leq 1$$
- Definite.
  $$TV(\mathbb{P}_{\theta}, \mathbb{P}_{\theta^*})=0 \Longleftrightarrow \mathbb{P}_{\theta}= \mathbb{P}_{\theta^*}$$
- Triangle inequality.
  $$TV(\mathbb{P}_{\theta}, \mathbb{P}_{\theta^*})\leq TV(\mathbb{P}_{\theta}, \mathbb{P}_{\theta'})+TV(\mathbb{P}_{\theta'}, \mathbb{P}_{\theta^*})$$
- Unclear how to build an estimator of TV from data.
- A **distance**.

### 2.2 Kullback-Leibler divergence

#### 2.2.1 Discreet & continuous case

$$\begin{aligned}
KL(\mathbb{P}_{\theta^*}, \mathbb{P}_{\theta})&=\sum\limits_{x \in E}p_{\theta^*}(x)\log\left(\frac{p_{\theta}(x)}{p_{\theta^*}(x)}\right)\\
&=\mathbf{E}_{\theta}\left[\log\left(\frac{p_{\theta}(X)}{p_{\theta^*}(X)}\right)\right]\\
&=\mathbf{E}\left[\log p_{\theta}(X)\right] - \mathbf{E}\left[\log p_{\theta^*}(X)\right]
\end{aligned}$$

$$\begin{aligned}
KL(\mathbb{P}_{\theta}, \mathbb{P}_{\theta^*})&=\int\limits_{x \in E}f_{\theta}(x)\log\left(\frac{f_{\theta}(x)}{f_{\theta^*}(x)}\right)dx\\
&=\mathbf{E}\left[\log\left(\frac{f_{\theta}(X)}{f_{\theta^*}(X)}\right)\right]\\
&=\mathbf{E}\left[\log f_{\theta}(X)\right] - \mathbf{E}\left[\log f_{\theta^*}(X)\right]
\end{aligned}$$

#### 2.2.2 Property

- Also known as relative entropy.
- Not symmetric in general.
- Positive.
  $$KL(\mathbb{P}_{\theta}, \mathbb{P}_{\theta^*}) \geq 0$$
- Definite.
  $$KL(\mathbb{P}_{\theta}, \mathbb{P}_{\theta^*})=0 \Longleftrightarrow \mathbb{P}_{\theta}= \mathbb{P}_{\theta^*}$$
- No triangle inequality.
- Not a **distance**, a **divergence**.

> The asymmetry reflects a sense of **suject**. For $KL(A, B)$, $A$ is the goal to reach and be compared with, whereas $B$ is the one the to compare.  
> 

#### 2.2.3 KL and MLE

Consider building an estimator $\widehat{KL}$ from $n$ i.i.d. r.v. $X_1, X_2, ..., X_n$.

> $$\mathbf{E}[const] = const$$
>
> - The probability measure of $\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(X)$ is a r.v. because $\theta$ is a r.v.. There could be infinite kind of probability measures for $\theta\in\Theta$
> - The probability measure of $\mathbb{P}_{\theta^*}(X)$ is constant because $\theta^*$ is constant.
> - Another way of thinking about this. $p_{\theta}(X)$ is a function of $(\theta, X)$. $\mathbf{E}[f(X, \theta)]$ is a function of only $\theta$. If $\theta$ is constant, then $\mathbf{E}[f(X, \theta)]$ is a constant.

$$
\begin{aligned}
KL(\mathbb{P}_{\theta^*}, \mathbb{P}_{\theta^*})
&=\mathbf{E}_{\theta^*}[\log(p_{\theta^*}(X))] - \mathbf{E}_{\theta^*}[\log(p_{\theta}(X))]\\
& = const - \mathbf{E}_{\theta^*}[\log(\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(X))]\\
\end{aligned}
$$

Let $Y=\log(\mathbb{P}_{\theta}(X))$. According to LLN, $\mathbf{E}_{\theta^*}[Y]$ can be sampled by $\bar{Y}$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{KL}(\mathbb{P}_{\theta^*}, \mathbb{P}_{\theta})
& = const - \frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_n\log\left(p_{\theta^*}(X_i)\right)\\
& = const -\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\prod\limits_np_{\theta^*}(X_i)\right)\\
\end{aligned}
$$
Drawing $\theta$ closer to $\theta^*$ is equivalent to **minimizing** KL divergence.

$$\begin{aligned}
    \min\limits_{\theta\in\Theta}\widehat{KL}(\mathbb{P}_{\theta^*}, \mathbb{P}_{\theta})&\Leftrightarrow\min\limits_{\theta\in\Theta}-\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\prod\limits_np_{\theta^*}(X_i)\right)\\
    &\Leftrightarrow\max\limits_{\theta\in\Theta}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\prod\limits_np_{\theta^*}(X_i)\right)\\
    &\Leftrightarrow\max\limits_{\theta\in\Theta}\prod\limits_np_{\theta^*}(X_i)\\
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore, **minimizing** KL divergence is equivalent to **maximizing** likelihood.
